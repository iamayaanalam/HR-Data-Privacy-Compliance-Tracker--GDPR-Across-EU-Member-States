HR Data Privacy Compliance Tracker- GDPR Across EU Member States

This research is a legal and compliance research repository analyzing the implementation of GDPR in relation to HR data processing in eight EU Member States. This research does not see GDPR as a homogeneous regulation but explains how national derogations in Article 9(4) of GDPR make the compliance obligations of organizations very different when doing business in Europe.

The countries covered in the research are Germany, France, The Netherlands, Ireland, Spain, Italy, Poland, and Sweden. The research focuses on six main types of HR data that include recruitment data, biometric data, employee surveillance, health data, performance data, and payroll data. A Compliance Risk Score has been established to assess differences in enforcement, documentation, and national legal complexity.

Project Objectives:

The main goal of this database was to answer the following practical compliance question: Is it possible for multinational companies to have one common GDPR HR policy in all the EU Member States?

After studying the regulatory guidance, national supervisory authorities, legal actions, and actual court cases, it turns out that it is not possible.

Though GDPR provides a unified legal framework, there are obligations which each Member State imposes, making compliance completely different. The same practice in different countries can be completely legal or subject to sanctions.

Research Methodology:

•	The database was created by means of manual research and analysis using:

•	Guidance from the European Data Protection Board (EDPB)

•	National Data Protection Authorities (CNIL, AEPD, AP, DPC, IMY, UODO, Garante, BfDI)

•	GDPR legal text (Art. 9, etc.)

•	Legitimate commentary

•	Seven actual enforcement cases

•	Excel for data modeling

•	Power BI for data visualization

•	DAX and LaTeX for analysis

The compliance risk score (0-5) was defined by means of a weighted index comprising three categories:

•	Enforcement Stringency (40%)

•	Complexity of National Derogations (30%)

•	Documentation Difficulty (30%)
Such an approach allows making direct comparisons across jurisdictions on the basis of factual legal information.

Key Findings:

Some of the key findings were as follows:

•	France was the highest-risk jurisdiction (5.0/5), owing to its stringent requirement for biometric approval and extensive documentational requirements.

•	The next was the Netherlands (4.4/5), where the concept of consent is usually invalid when it comes to employment-sensitive data.

•	Lowest risk was reported by Ireland (1.3/5), but the report clarifies that less enforcement does not indicate fewer legal obligations.
Cases of high-profile enforcement actions included:

•	Fine of €2 million for unlawful criminal record check during hiring process (Spain).

•	Fine of €725,000 associated with biometric attendance systems (Netherlands).

•	Fine of €420,000 for WhatsApp communications during disciplinary processes (Italy).
It becomes evident from these cases that the choice of legal basis, documentation, and national legislations often affect compliance more than the processing quantity.

Development of the Dashboard:

Besides the report, a Power BI dashboard was created to help make decisions using the findings from the legal research.

The dashboard contains:

•	Heatmaps for Compliance risk scores

•	Country rankings

•	Comparison of GDPR fines

•	KPI summaries

•	Filtering options for different categories within HR data types
This allows for easy identification of high risk jurisdictions and comparison of obligations between several countries by using an interactive dashboard rather than legal documents.

Strategic Outcome:

The key finding of the research is that GDPR compliance cannot be achieved via a one-size-fits-all European HR policy.
On the contrary, companies need to:

•	Create category-specific compliance playbooks

•	Focus on high-risk jurisdictions like France and the Netherlands

•	Use documented legitimate interest assessments rather than employee consent by default

•	Adopt country-specific data retention schedules

•	Stay on top of regulatory changes in Member States
The project shows the value of integrating legal research, compliance, business intelligence, and governance in practice for multinational corporations.
